# Related Work and Defensible Novelty Boundary

**Status:** scoped literature positioning, not a claim of exhaustive novelty  
**Date:** 2026-08-21

## 1. Bottom line

The individual algorithms in OIA-1 sit near established work in automata testing, active hypothesis testing, decision-region determination, controlled model detection, POMDPs, and dual control. The defensible contribution is therefore **not** “the first adaptive distinguishing-sequence algorithm” or “the first decision-aware experiment planner.”

The present contribution is better described as an integrated audit protocol with explicit witness-only and fail-closed negative statuses for a particular scientific bottleneck:

> Before evaluating an executable ontology learner, determine whether the evaluator’s permitted interventions can identify the proposed ontology distinction at all, whether full identity is unnecessarily strong, whether the distinction changes a declared decision, and whether the probe remains worthwhile under world-dependent task loss.

A publishable novelty claim would still require a systematic review and an independently authored use case.

## 2. Adaptive and preset distinguishing sequences

The classic automata-testing problem asks how inputs and observed outputs can identify an unknown machine state. Preset distinguishing sequences fix the entire input word in advance; adaptive distinguishing sequences select the next input from prior outputs and therefore form a decision tree. This literature already establishes that adaptive and preset existence differ and that optimal synthesis can be computationally difficult.

OIA-1 directly inherits this structure. Its departures are mainly in framing and composition:

- the uncertain worlds may belong to different executable candidate machines rather than different states of one known machine;
- target labels may be coarser than identity;
- the output is used as a pre-evaluation ontology-identifiability audit; and
- exact decision and task-loss layers are reported alongside separation.

These are useful distinctions, but not yet evidence of a new core ADS theorem.

## 3. Active automata learning

Active automata learning infers a behaviorally equivalent automaton by querying an unknown system, commonly through membership and approximated equivalence queries. OIA-1 does not learn a machine from a live oracle. It assumes a frozen finite candidate set and asks whether a declared intervention interface can adjudicate among those candidates.

This makes OIA-1 closer to **candidate-set diagnosis before or during bounded external execution** than to active automata learning itself. A substrate-specific adapter now connects one public CPython codec family to the audit, but candidate generation and ontology learning remain outside the v0.2 claim.

## 4. Decision-region determination

Bayesian active learning for decision making has explicitly argued that one need not identify the complete hypothesis when several hypotheses support the same decision. Decision-region approaches seek tests that move posterior support into a single valid decision region.

OIA-1’s target-label and common-optimum modes strongly overlap with this idea. Its exact finite-state version differs in that:

- actions may alter candidate internal state;
- output laws are generated by executable stateful candidates;
- impossibility is certified over the complete reachable belief graph; and
- candidate-dependent task consequences are retained as an outcome vector.

Again, the safest claim is integrated exact auditing for this executable-candidate setting, not invention of decision-region testing.

## 5. Controlled model and MDP detection

Work on detecting which MDP governs a controlled process already studies policy existence and synthesis when the chosen policy changes the observed history. That literature is a direct neighbor and prevents any broad claim that policy-dependent model detection is new.

OIA-1 is narrower in stochastic scope and broader in its audit outputs:

- v0.2 is deterministic finite-state;
- it reports behavioral equivalence, pairwise words, adaptive and preset target identification, decision common-optima, and task-loss policy differences in one artifact;
- it produces finite closed losing-set certificates; and
- it is intended as an outcome-free gate for ontology-revision experiments.

## 6. Sequential certification at minimum task loss

A 2026 preprint by Zijie Huang develops a sequential theory of representation-adequacy certification in the currency of task loss. It defines a decision-theoretic adequacy label, derives fixed-kernel information/task-loss bounds, and explicitly identifies policy switching and representation repair as outside its proved model.

The overlap with OIA-1 is substantial:

- both ask whether evidence about representation adequacy is worth its task consequence;
- both allow action loss to depend on the unknown environment; and
- both distinguish hopeless, free, and costly information regimes.

The scopes differ:

- Huang studies stochastic fixed-confidence certification with fixed action channels and asymptotic task-loss bounds;
- OIA-1 v0.2 studies exact finite deterministic stateful candidate semantics, bounded policy trees, target classes, and executable impossibility certificates;
- OIA-1 does **not** yet modify the representation, so it does not solve Huang’s repair problem; and
- OIA-1’s candidate state means the effective next observation law depends on prior actions, but this remains a standard controlled-state/POMDP phenomenon rather than a general new theory of moving representations.

The recent preprint therefore sharpens, rather than eliminates, the niche: OIA-1 can serve as an exact finite-state laboratory and pre-audit for a future necessary-revision experiment, but it should not claim the general theory of certification under representation repair.

## 7. POMDP and dual-control interpretation

The hidden candidate and its internal state form a finite hidden world. Actions both reveal information and alter future states, while terminal utilities and intervention losses define a control objective. This is naturally expressible as a deterministic finite POMDP or contingent-planning problem.

The scientific value of OIA-1 must therefore come from the ontology-evaluation contract and the exact artifacts it demands, not from pretending the belief-state reduction is new.

## 8. Candidate contribution statement

A conservative contribution statement for v0.2 is:

> OIA-1 formalizes an outcome-free identifiability gate for finite executable ontology-candidate sets. It composes exact behavioral quotienting, pairwise and multi-candidate intervention synthesis, target/decision-region identification, verifiable positive and negative certificates, and candidate-dependent task-loss optimization in an audit report that separates verified capped witnesses from exact minima and exact impossibility. Its purpose is to determine, before sealed evaluation, whether a proposed ontology revision is behaviorally testable and decision-relevant through the declared interface.

The CPython Track A slice adds opened external instrument validation—adapter fidelity, prospective policy execution, and outside-model detection—but no sealed ontology-identification or decision-superiority result.

## 9. What could become genuinely new

The strongest next research contribution would not be another generic policy solver. It would be a preregistered **necessary executable revision certificate** that composes four facts:

1. a frozen base representation cannot express a target-relevant behavioral partition;
2. an independently proposed executable extension can express it;
3. evaluator-private interventions identify the new distinction prospectively; and
4. the identified distinction changes and improves a predeclared decision beyond strong black-box and model-free baselines.

OIA-1 addresses the identifiability, adapter-fidelity, and decision-analysis portions of that chain. Necessary revision, sealed transfer, and superiority remain unshown.
